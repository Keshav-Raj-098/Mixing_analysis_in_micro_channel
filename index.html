🔬 Research Objectives & Physical Conclusions

  • How Simple Changes to Geometry Alter Mixing: In microchannels where Pe > 1000, 1D molecular diffusion across a single flat interface (Y-mixer) is hopelessly slow and independent of flow rate. Opposed 180° impingement (T-mixer) induces vortex engulfment at Re ≥ 100 boosting mixing to ~33%, but viscous dissipation in the straight channel limits downstream gains. In contrast, 3D Split-and-Recombine (SR) geometry repeatedly splits and interleaves fluid layers (Baker's transformation: 2n lamellae). Cutting diffusion distance in half accelerates diffusion by 4× (τ ∼ w2/D), while curvature induces Dean vortices that maintain high concentration gradients throughout the channel.
  • The Most Effective Design: The SR-Mixer is overwhelmingly the most effective geometry, achieving up to 79.5% mixing (2.4× higher than T-mixer and 5.5× higher than Y-mixer).
  • Operational Sweet-Spot: Operating the SR-mixer at Re = 50 to 100 delivers an impressive 62.0% – 65.4% mixing with an extremely modest pressure drop of only 5.0 – 14.4 kPa (0.05 – 0.14 bar) and negligible pumping power (< 0.4 mW), maximizing cost-benefit mixing energy efficiency (η = 0.045 – 0.123 %/kPa).
